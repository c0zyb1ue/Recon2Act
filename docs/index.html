Abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are promising for general-purpose robot manipulation, but adapting them to a specific real-world workspace remains difficult due to the cost of real demonstration collection and the persistent sim-to-real gap. We study a real-to-sim-to-real pipeline for fine-tuning a pi0.5-based VLA policy using a reconstructed digital twin. A tabletop manipulation scene is captured from multiple viewpoints, reconstructed with VGGT COLMAP, refined with MiLO, imported into Isaac Sim, and used to generate simulated demonstrations. The resulting policy is evaluated in simulation and deployed on the real robot, revealing both the potential of digital-twin-based VLA training and the remaining challenges in real-world transfer.

Observations

Digital-twin-based simulation provides a scalable way to generate task demonstrations, but robust real-world deployment remains challenging. We observed limitations from the current VLA policy capability, visual and physical sim-to-real discrepancies, and joint-level error accumulation in the LeRobot execution pipeline.
